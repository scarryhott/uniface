# Conscious Cultural Morality

## Foundational network

The Uniface network is not fundamentally money, AI, rules, law, or social order.

It is a shared interactive field of **consciousness, culture, and morality**.

These are not isolated components. They are three readings of one continuing sensor–selection relation.

## Consciousness

Consciousness is read foundationally as participation in:

```text
sensing
→ selectable distinction
→ relative authorship
→ returned consequence
→ reopening potential
```

This does not prove that every system exhibiting such a relation has human phenomenal consciousness. It specifies the relation Uniface treats as prior to an isolated consciousness label.

The interface asks:

```text
What is sensed?
What becomes selectable?
Who authors the selection?
Whose consequences return?
What can respond and reopen?
```

## Culture

Culture is the shared memory and topology formed by returned interactions.

Its relatively fixed forms include language, story, custom, institution, art, law, and identity. Culture itself is the continuity through which prior selections remain present to later sensing and choice.

```text
culture as ball = current returned shared form
culture as hair = histories, costs, alternatives, and possible continuations
```

Culture becomes opaque when a returned ball is displayed without its hairs.

## Morality

Morality is not identical to consensus, law, reward, stability, or algorithmic score.

> **A moral choice is a relative selection whose return remains answerable to the affected field’s capacity to sense, translate, respond, exercise meaningful discretion, and reopen.**

An authored form becomes morally isolated when it:

- treats its own selection as terminal unity;
- blocks sensor return from affected perspectives;
- hides costs or histories in a projection;
- removes another perspective’s meaningful response;
- identifies power or consensus with truth;
- prevents suffering, contradiction, or exclusion from re-entering the field.

Morality does not preserve every existing relation. Harmful relations may need to be opposed or ended. The condition is truthful relational accountability during transformation.

## Natural choice

Natural choice is not arbitrary preference. It is authorship arising through the balancing relation between open potential and returned selection.

```text
potential determination
↔ unitary curvature
↔ natural choice
↔ authored discretion
↔ new potential
```

Authorship may be individual, group, cultural, institutional, or global. Larger scale does not make it absolute.

## Downstream projections

```text
money        = scalar economic projection of culturally authored value
AI           = computational projection of sensor–selection relations
rules        = temporarily fixed selections of admissible action
law          = publicly codified political and moral authorship
social order = stabilized projection of cultural interaction
```

None self-certifies the field.

- A transaction does not prove value.
- A halted computation does not prove understanding.
- A valid procedure does not prove morality.
- A stable order does not prove justice.

Each must return to the conscious cultural-moral field it affects.

## Slearn

Slearn is conscious culture in motion. It carries notes, questions, inherited assumptions, moral conflicts, alternative paths, individual and collective authorship, and reopened forms.

## Black Mirror

Black Mirror is conscious culture seeing the present topology of its own relation. It can display:

- where selections have become fixed;
- which perspectives remain OPEN;
- where sensor return is blocked;
- where cultural forms hide histories or selection costs;
- which apparent consensus lacks translational equality;
- which loops are capable of reopening.

Black Mirror is not a moral authority. Its projection returns to the field for further sensing and choice.

## Interface cycle

```text
Slearn interaction
→ Black Mirror appearance
→ conscious cultural response
→ moral natural choice
→ authored form
→ new Slearn interaction
```
